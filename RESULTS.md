# Model Results

These results were generated by running `run_analysis.py` on the official UCI Default of Credit Card Clients dataset.

## Dataset snapshot

- 30,000 customer records
- 24 columns after target standardization
- 0 missing cells
- 35 duplicate rows detected
- Overall default rate: approximately 22.12%

## Model comparison at threshold 0.50

| Model | ROC-AUC | Average Precision | Precision (Default) | Recall (Default) | F1 (Default) | Brier Score |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Logistic Regression | 0.7476 | 0.4979 | 0.4414 | 0.6014 | 0.5091 | 0.1934 |
| Random Forest | **0.7730** | **0.5509** | **0.5519** | 0.5207 | **0.5359** | **0.1489** |

Random Forest is the stronger overall challenger because it improves ranking performance, precision, F1 and probability accuracy. Logistic Regression retains slightly higher default recall at the default 0.50 threshold.

## Operating-threshold decision

A threshold of 0.50 is not automatically the correct business cutoff. The cost of missing a future defaulter can differ from the cost of reviewing or restricting a non-defaulter.

A useful comparison is:

| Model / threshold | Precision | Recall | Flag rate | False positives | True positives |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Logistic Regression @ 0.50 | 44.14% | 60.14% | 30.13% | 1,010 | 798 |
| Random Forest @ 0.40 | **48.17%** | **59.53%** | **27.33%** | **850** | 790 |

Random Forest at 0.40 captures nearly the same proportion of defaulters as Logistic Regression at 0.50, while improving precision and reducing false positives by 160 customers in the test set. For this portfolio case study, **0.40 is therefore a defensible illustrative operating point** when the goal is to retain roughly 60% default recall while reducing unnecessary flags.

This is not a production lending policy. A real threshold should be selected using explicit financial costs, portfolio capacity, regulation and validation on out-of-time data.

## Risk-band performance

Random Forest PD estimates were grouped into four illustrative risk bands:

| Risk band | Customers | Average predicted PD | Observed default rate | Portfolio share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | 753 | 6.71% | 5.18% | 12.55% |
| Medium | 2,309 | 17.46% | 11.09% | 38.48% |
| High | 1,686 | 34.23% | 20.23% | 28.10% |
| Very High | 1,252 | 70.61% | **55.19%** | 20.87% |

Observed default rates rise monotonically from Low to Very High risk, showing useful separation for portfolio segmentation. The Very High group has an observed default rate more than ten times the Low group.

## Most important Random Forest features

Top model-importance features include:

1. `months_delayed`
2. `pay_0` — most recent repayment status
3. `max_delay`
4. `utilization_proxy`
5. `payment_bill_ratio`
6. `avg_payment`
7. `bill_amt1`
8. `avg_bill`
9. `limit_bal`
10. `pay_amt1`

The dominant signal is repayment behavior and delinquency history, supplemented by balance/utilization and payment behavior. Random Forest feature importance is **not directional and not causal**; it indicates model reliance, not that changing a feature would necessarily change default risk by that amount.

## Portfolio conclusion

The project demonstrates a complete credit-risk workflow rather than only fitting a classifier:

- Probability of Default modeling
- interpretable baseline vs nonlinear challenger
- discrimination and calibration evaluation
- threshold trade-off analysis
- risk segmentation
- model explainability
- reproducibility, tests and governance documentation

The strongest portfolio story is that **Random Forest improved overall predictive quality, while threshold tuning recovered almost all of the baseline model's default recall with fewer false positives**.
